1. Maximal-Temperature Origin (High-Entropy Pole)
At the start of any cycle (\(\lambda=0\)):
\[
\tau(0)\;=\;2\pi\approx6.283185.
\]
The spiral point lies at maximal radius:
\[
\begin{align*}
x(0)&\;=\;2\pi\cdot\cos(2\pi)\;=\;2\pi,\\
y(0)&\;=\;2\pi\cdot\sin(2\pi)\;=\;0.
\end{align*}
\]
This is the high-entropy origin. The softmax is at its softest Arcan scale; probability mass is most widely distributed across the permitted vocabulary face while the restricted face remains geometrically suppressed by the prior steering. In the hierarchy this point is the outermost terminus of the spiral—the thermodynamic “north pole” of the cycle.
2. Continuous Cooling Arm (the Spiral Descent)
As \(\lambda\) advances from 0 toward 1 the temperature falls linearly:
\[
\tau(\lambda)\;=\;2\pi(1-\lambda)\quad\downarrow\quad0.
\]
Simultaneously the argument of the trigonometric functions also shrinks, so the point \((x(\lambda),y(\lambda))\) traces an Archimedean spiral that winds inward. Three geometric facts characterise the cooling arm:
Radial coordinate \(r(\lambda)=\tau(\lambda)\) is identical to the instantaneous temperature. Every point on the spiral therefore carries an explicit thermodynamic label.
Angular coordinate advances with \(\tau(\lambda)\) itself; the spiral is self-parameterised by the cooling law.
Entropy collapse is continuous: the softmax hardens in exact proportion to the decreasing radius. Probability mass concentrates onto the dominant permitted tokens while restricted-token mass is driven toward zero.
The cooling arm is the body of the hierarchy—the continuous trajectory that realises the thermodynamic transition from maximal softness to near-greedy collapse.
3. Entropy-Collapse Target (the Origin)
At \(\lambda=1\):
\[
\tau(1)\;=\;0,\qquad
(x,y)\;=\;(0,0).
\]
The spiral has reached the origin. In the softmax this corresponds to the near-greedy limit: virtually all probability mass occupies the single highest-logit permitted token and the restricted face is extinguished. The origin is the entropy-collapse target—the innermost terminus of the spiral hierarchy.
4. 360° Cycle Closure and Residual Offset
Because the angular parameter is itself a full turn of \(\tau\), the moment \(\lambda\) returns to 0 (or equivalently when a new cycle begins) the temperature is restored exactly:
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{reset}}\;=\;2\pi.
\]
The spiral jumps from the origin back to the maximal-radius point \((2\pi,0)\). This jump is the 360° cycle closure.
The closure is not perfectly periodic. The seventh-multiple residual
\[
\delta\approx0.0051676\,\mathrm{rad}
\]
is inherited as a comoving angular offset. Consequently each subsequent spiral begins at a phase-shifted azimuth:
\[
\begin{align*}
x_n(\lambda)&\;=\;\tau(\lambda)\cdot\cos\bigl(\tau(\lambda)+n\delta\bigr),\\
y_n(\lambda)&\;=\;\tau(\lambda)\cdot\sin\bigl(\tau(\lambda)+n\delta\bigr).
\end{align*}
\]
The hierarchy therefore consists of a family of nested, phase-offset spirals—one spiral per completed 360° cycle—whose successive outer termini are separated by the residual \(\delta\).
